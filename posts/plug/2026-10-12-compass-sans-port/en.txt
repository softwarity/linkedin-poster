Your MongoDB runs inside the cluster, no port is exposed, and all you want is to open it in Compass. A port-forward? Change the stack? There's a simpler way.

plug runs a local process as if it were inside your Docker, Compose, Swarm or Kubernetes cluster.

A real case: I need to check a few documents in the dev database. MongoDB is right there, reachable by the services under the name mongodb, but nothing is exposed to the outside. Usually that means opening a port-forward, or publishing a port in the stack… and remembering to remove it afterwards.

With plug, on my Mac:

plug -c open -a Compass

Compass connects to mongodb:27017, by name, like any other service in the cluster. No localhost:PORT, no /etc/hosts.

The -c flag is pure client mode: the process reaches services by name, but nothing is named in the cluster and no port is reserved on the agent. It works for any tool that only consumes: a database GUI, a one-off script, or a simple:

plug -c psql -h postgres

I close Compass: my machine is exactly as it was.

On Linux, it's plug -c mongodb-compass; on Windows, you pass the path to the Compass executable.

The source code is on GitHub: https://github.com/softwarity/plug
Docs: https://plug.softwarity.dev
