API gateway, app-gateway: the I becomes a P. A single letter, but a different idea of what sits in front of your services.

Meerkat is a single door, with access control, in front of your company's internal applications. Softwarity calls this an app-gateway.

A real case: a team builds an internal application as microservices. At first, an API gateway in front of the services is enough. Then come sign-in pages, SSO, a second factor. Then roles and tenants, per-route access rules, quotas, an audit trail, TLS, OpenAPI docs for each service, a tunnel for developers, dashboards. The stack grows around the gateway: an IAM, an authentication proxy, configuration files everywhere, every brick shipping its own telemetry. And the organization screens? You build those yourself.

That's because the API gateway was designed to expose APIs to third parties: a key per partner, billing per call.

An app-gateway starts from the other end: a single door in front of one application and its users.
→ accounts, roles, tenants and sign-in pages are part of the product, passkeys and second factor included
→ since everything goes through it, it's also the right place for audit, metrics and traces
→ your services receive requests that are already authenticated, with the identity in headers or in a signed JWT: who is calling, their roles, their tenant. They no longer carry a sign-in page, a role model or a users table.

One image, no dependencies: no database beside it, no cache, no message broker. The Community image weighs 70 MB.

To try it, use the evaluation edition: all of Enterprise, free, with no time limit, just a notice on screen.

docker run -p 8080:8080 -p 9090:9090 -e MEERKAT_ADMIN_PASSWORD=choose-one softwarity/meerkat

Port 8080 is for your users, 9090 for the console. The Community edition (softwarity/meerkat-ce) is free, production included.
